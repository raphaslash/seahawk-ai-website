Our mission

The students who learn to steer AI won't be the ones replaced by it.

High school is where that head start gets built. Seahawk AI exists so students at every level — coders and non-coders alike — get real hands on real tools: prompting, generating, designing, automating, and building things people actually use. We learn out loud, argue about the hard parts, and leave every meeting having made something.

Why we exist

Purpose, mission, and the part people skip

Purpose

Give students a head start

The skills that matter in five years are being invented right now. We'd rather learn them early than catch up late.

Mission

Adapt, then control

Not to fear AI and not to worship it — to understand it well enough to direct it. Every meeting moves someone one step closer.

★ The hard part

Ask the uncomfortable questions

Is it cheating? Whose work trained it? What happens to the jobs? We debate it openly instead of pretending it's settled.

The founders

Two students, one bet
on what comes next.

Rapha (left) & Rayyan Club fair, 2025
Started with a poster board, a bag of candy, and a QR code. It worked.

Raphael Osinovsky and Rayyan Rahman started Seahawk AI in 2025 because AI is rapidly changing nearly every industry, yet students aren't being taught how to use it effectively.

They had both been using AI as a learning and building tool. While some students dismissed it entirely and others used it as a homework machine, they saw something different: AI could be a tool for creating, experimenting, and actually building things.

So they made the club they wanted to join.

Every other Monday, students show up and leave having made something real: an image, a video, a prompt library, an app, or a skill that saves hours of work with AI. No lecture-and-leave. No prerequisites. Just a room full of people learning from each other and getting genuinely good at one of the most important tools of their generation.
